The Quote Sandwich

Never drop a quote on its own! Always introduce it, state it, and cite it properly using MLA format.

1. The Top Bun (Signal Phrase)

Introduce the quote by providing context or naming the author.

According to the text,

2. The Meat (The Quote)

Copy the exact words from the source, wrapped in quotation marks.

"bionic limbs respond to electrical signals from muscles"

3. The Bottom Bun (Citation)

Include the parenthetical citation and end punctuation AFTER the parentheses.

(Smith 42).

Final Assembled Sentence:

According to the text, "bionic limbs respond to electrical signals from muscles" (Smith 42).

Capitalization (M.I.N.T.S.)

  • M
    Months, Days, Holidays (e.g., October)
  • I
    The pronoun "I" (e.g., She and I went)
  • N
    Names of people, places (e.g., Sarah)
  • T
    Titles of books, movies (e.g., The Matrix)
  • S
    Start of every sentence!

Graphic Organizers

A structural roadmap for academic writing. Follow these steps to build a bulletproof paragraph.

T

Topic Sentence

Directly answer the prompt. State your main claim clearly without saying "I think".

I

Important Evidence

Provide a quote or specific detail from the text that proves your topic sentence.

D

Detailed Analysis

The "So What?". Explain why your evidence matters and how it connects back to your claim.

E

Ending Sentence

Wrap up the paragraph. Restate your main idea using different words.

Reading Source

Bionics: The Future of Physical Therapy

1

Bionics involves using mechanical or electronic devices to replace human body parts. Modern bionic limbs are advanced; they can be shunted directly to the nervous system, allowing patients to control them with their minds. This has revolutionized prostheses and dramatically improved physical therapy outcomes.

2

In the past, physical therapy for amputees often involved learning to use rudimentary, heavy prosthetics that offered limited mobility. Today, patients work with bionic limbs that respond to electrical signals from their own muscles. This connection makes movement feel more natural and intuitive.

3

However, the transition isn't entirely seamless. Learning to use a bionic limb still requires rigorous physical therapy to retrain the brain to communicate with the new device. Despite the challenges, the benefits are profound, offering individuals a renewed sense of independence and capability.
