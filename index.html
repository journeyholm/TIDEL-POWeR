Before we write

You are about to build a paragraph out of someone else's ideas.

Not copy it. Build it — plan it, organize it, write it, then make it stronger. Real writers almost never start with sentence one. They start with a process. That process is what this lab teaches you, and it works on any text you will ever be handed: a science article, a novel, a primary source, a test prompt.

Foreword

What writing actually is: The Writing Rope

Writing looks like one skill. It isn't. Literacy researcher Joan Sedita describes skilled writing as a rope — five separate strands twisted together. When writing feels hard, it is almost never "I'm bad at writing." It is usually one strand that needs work. Tap each strand to see what it does.

Which strands you're working today

This process works mostly on Critical Thinking (what do I want to say?) and Text Structure (how do I arrange it?). Those are the two strands the POWeR Cycle is built to strengthen. You'll touch Syntax and Writing Craft in the mini-lessons, and Transcription when you edit.

The Process

The POWeR Cycle — five moves, every time

Every text gets the same treatment. That is the whole point: once you know these five moves, the text stops mattering. You are not learning "the bionics paragraph." You are learning a process you will run for the rest of your life.

The 80 / 20 rule

The first time through a process, someone else does about 80% of the thinking out loud and you do 20%. As you get stronger that flips: you do 80%. This lab works the same way — your first text walks beside you and explains every move. After that, the lessons step back and you drive.

Prepare to write

Power up your self-talk 🔋

Writing is the hardest thing we ask your brain to do. It asks you to think of an idea, hold it in your memory, choose words for it, spell those words, and punctuate them — all at once. So of course it feels hard. Feeling stuck is not evidence that you are a bad writer. It's evidence that you're writing.

The move

Skilled writers don't avoid the stuck feeling — they talk themselves through it. That inner voice is a tool you can actually change. Below are things students really say when writing gets hard. Your job: swap each one for something that keeps you working.

Quote Lab · MLA

How to use somebody else's words 📎

Borrowing a sentence from a text is the single most technical thing you do as a writer. There are rules for how you introduce it, rules for the punctuation, and rules for the credit you give. Get them right and your paragraph sounds like it was written by someone older. Get them wrong and it looks like you copied.

Plan · Pre-read

Read it once. For real.

This first read has no job attached — no highlighting, no notes, no organizer. Just read it the way you'd read something interesting. Writers who skip this read end up writing about the one sentence they happened to notice instead of what the text is actually about.
